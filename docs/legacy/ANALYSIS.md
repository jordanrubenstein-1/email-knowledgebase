# Email Performance Analysis
> 3,221 campaigns across 6 brands (1,000+ delivered sends) | July 2024 – December 2025

---

## Executive Summary

### Top 5 Actionable Insights
1. **Cart abandonment emails outperform everything** — 3.00% click rate vs 0.92% average (3.3x lift)
2. **"You/Your" in subject lines correlates with success** — +2.2pt open rate, 2.7x more common in top performers
3. **Holiday tentpoles underperform baseline** — July 4th (-0.58pt), Christmas (-0.36pt), BFCM (-0.16pt) — likely inbox competition
4. **Text-only emails perform well when used strategically** — BUR's letter-style emails stand out; novelty may be the driver
5. **August is the sleeper month** — 1.72% click rate, highest of any month (back-to-school timing)

## Critical Findings Summary

### What Works
1. **Personalization** — "You/Your" in subjects, "Items in your design" emails, cart abandonment
2. **Editorial content** — Educational tips outperform sales 3x (HAV Hideaway series)
3. **Minimal imagery** — 0-2 images and 0-2 links maximize clicks
4. **August timing** — Best month across multiple brands
5. **Wednesday evening** — HAV's sweet spot (21:00 UTC)

### What Correlates with Lower Performance
*Note: These are correlations, not necessarily causes. Overused tactics may underperform due to fatigue, not inherent weakness.*

1. **Holiday tentpoles** — All underperform baseline (likely due to inbox competition, not message quality)
2. **Urgency language** — 32% in bottom vs 18% in top performers (possibly overused, triggering fatigue)
3. **ALL CAPS words** — -4.4pt open rate (may signal spam or desperation)
4. **Percent discounts in subject** — -3.4pt open rate (generic; everyone does this)
5. **Aspirational tone** — 46% of emails use it, lowest click rate (overused = invisible)
6. **Generic sale emails** — 0.80% click vs 3.00% for cart abandonment (no personalization)

### Brand-Specific Patterns to Investigate
These correlations suggest areas for testing, not absolute rules. Performance may depend on frequency, sequence, and context:

| Brand | Pattern | Observed Impact | Possible Explanation |
|-------|---------|-----------------|----------------------|
| BUR | Text-only outperforms visual | 3.20% vs 0.11% | Personal letter style is novel; stands out from visual competitors |
| CZ | ALL CAPS underperforms | -11.6pt open | May signal "spam" or appear aggressive to this audience |
| ID | Emojis underperform | -6.1pt open | Audience may perceive as less premium/professional |
| STF | Editorial underperforms | 0.30% click | Product-focused audience wants to shop, not read |
| HAV | Category nav underperforms | 0.11% click | Editorial readers want content, not navigation |

**Caution:** If everyone adopted the "winning" pattern, it would likely stop working. The value may be in differentiation, not the tactic itself.

## Methodology & Limitations

### What This Analysis Can Tell You
- **Correlations** between tactics and performance
- **Patterns** that warrant A/B testing
- **Baseline benchmarks** for comparison
- **Audience differences** between brands

### What This Analysis Cannot Tell You
- **Causation** — We see correlation, not cause. A tactic that correlates with success may work because it's rare, not because it's inherently better.
- **Novelty effects** — Text-only emails may outperform because they're uncommon. If 80% of emails became text-only, the pattern would likely reverse.
- **Sequence effects** — A promotional email after 5 content emails may perform differently than after 5 other promos.
- **Selection bias** — Certain tactics may only be used in specific contexts (e.g., text-only for founder letters) that inherently perform better.
- **Audience fatigue** — "Urgency words" may have worked well 5 years ago; their current underperformance may reflect overuse, not inherent weakness.

### Recommended Approach
1. **Use findings as hypotheses** — Test before assuming causation
2. **Consider frequency** — A winning tactic used sparingly beats a winning tactic used constantly
3. **Test sequences** — Vary the mix, not just individual emails
4. **Monitor for decay** — What works today may stop working as competitors adopt it
5. **Segment analysis** — These are averages; your highest-value segments may behave differently
