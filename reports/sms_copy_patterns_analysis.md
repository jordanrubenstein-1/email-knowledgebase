## Cross-Brand Patterns

### What Works Across All Three Brands:
1. **Personalization** (ID: "your"/"you", SF: "you"/"your") - though CZ shows "you" overused in bottom performers
2. **Specificity** - Product-specific terms (ID: "chairs", CZ: "favorites", "weave", "ornament")
3. **Action/Urgency** - (ID: "tomorrow", CZ: "wait", "disappear", SF: "ready")

### What Doesn't Work:
1. **"sitewide"** - Appears in bottom performers for all three brands
2. **Generic time pressure** - "ends", "final", "hours", "week" (CZ bottom performers)
3. **Generic promotional language** - "cyber", generic "sale" messaging

### Brand-Specific Differences:
- **ID**: Black Friday messaging works when combined with personalization and specific products
- **CZ**: Scarcity language is highly effective; "you" can be overused
- **SF**: Strong performance with personalization + action language; fewer clear patterns due to smaller sample size

---

## Recommendations

1. **Avoid "sitewide"** - This term consistently underperforms across all brands
2. **Use specific product terms** - Instead of generic "sale" or "items", mention specific products
3. **Balance personalization** - "your"/"you" works well (ID, SF) but can be overused (CZ)
4. **Scarcity language** - Effective for CZ ("sold-out", "disappear"); test for other brands
5. **Action-oriented language** - "ready", "wait", "tomorrow" drive engagement
6. **Avoid generic time pressure** - "ends soon", "final hours" may create fatigue (CZ)
